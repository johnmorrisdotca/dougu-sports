## Trophies, medals and crests

The trophies are drawn in SVG for this package, in a simplified and generic form, from what the real objects are known to look like: their proportions and their parts, looked at in photographs that were never traced or copied (the contact sheets were compared with them by eye). Each famous trophy is described, not named: `trophy:hockey-cup` is a tall silver cup with a small flared bowl, a collar, and a barrel of five bands that each widen a little; `trophy:baseball` a ring of thirty gold pennants on poles, their tops stepping up once round, standing round a baseball on a round base; `trophy:football` a silver football leaning on one end on a tapered three-sided stand; `trophy:basketball` a gold ball over a rim and a net, on a tapered column and a stepped base; `trophy:canadian-bowl` a wide silver bowl with two handles on a stem over a tall conical base of panels; `trophy:soccer-globe` a gold globe held up by figures that spiral round a column that flares from a slim waist, over two green bands; `trophy:tennis-plate` a silver salver with rings of gilt and rounded panels; `trophy:tennis-cup` a gilded two-handled cup with a domed lid and a small pineapple; `trophy:cricket-urn` a small terracotta urn with square handles on a wooden base; `trophy:rugby-cup` a gilded two-handled cup with a lid. No ids, titles, docs or drawings carry a real trophy's name, logo, wordmark or engraving.

The medals' ribbon has five stripes in the colours of a games' five rings (blue, yellow, black, green, red), and no rings are drawn.

## Balls and gear

What the balls and implements are drawn from, and how the gear is drawn.

- **Sizes: the rulebooks.** For each ball the federation's own rulebook (not Wikipedia, not a shop) was read for the diameter or length, the circumference, the mass and what the rule fixes about the look, and the rule is cited in the ball's `size.rule`. The table is in NOTICE.md. Read 2026-10-14. Figures that are not in the rule are marked: the basketball 3x3's (the FIBA 3x3 appendix refused the request; the figures are a retailer's reproduction of its Table 1), the football of the IFAF (in a diagram, read from the rendered page), the Australian football's length (the Laws give two girths; 280 mm is the length of an ellipse of those), the hammer's wire (the table gives only a maximum overall length), the sepak takraw ball (the 2013 ISTAF law, the newest found), the padel ball (FIP's certification text that quotes the Rules of Padel), the softball (the WBSC Slow Pitch appendix read in English, the Fast Pitch one in the FFBS's French translation) and the water polo ball (World Aquatics' regulations, hosted by a national federation).
- **Where the rule says less than the brief did:** the pickleball rulebook does not tell indoor and outdoor balls apart (both are 26 to 40 holes; 26 large holes and 40 small ones is how they are sold); the cricket Laws set no colour (the ICC one-day conditions say white; no ICC text read says pink); the NFL ball is "natural tan", with laces and no stripes in the text; the CFL ball has 1-inch white stripes round the short axis, three inches from the widest point; the IFAF ball has two such stripes on the two panels beside the laces, eight laces and four panels; the padel ball may be white, yellow or any contrasting colour; the water polo ball's colour is not regulated; the squash specification names double yellow, yellow, green, red and blue dots and recommends them; the handball Ball Regulations ask for at least 32 panels on a leather ball; table-tennis balls are white or orange and matt; the discus has no indentations; a golf ball has no minimum mass and no maximum size.
- **The look: our own.** The soccer ball is the truncated icosahedron (12 pentagons, 20 hexagons) with the edges followed along the sphere; its coloured twin and the teqball ball have a motif and colours of our own, and no World Cup ball's pattern is used. The baseball, softball, tennis, padel and sliotar seam is the parametric curve x=(1-b)cos t + b cos 3t, y=(1-b)sin t - b sin 3t, z=2sqrt(b(1-b)) sin 2t (it lies on the unit sphere for every b). The volleyball's panels are the six faces of a cube, three strips each, mapped onto the sphere so that every seam is an arc of a great circle and the strips of neighbouring faces run square to each other; the 8-panel ball is the octants. The basketball's two curved channels, latitude = +-(0.304 + 0.585 sin^2 longitude), were fitted by least squares to the 1,766 seam pixels of a drawing and a photograph of a generic ball, which were looked at and not copied (a Wikimedia Commons clip art and a photograph, viewed on 2026-10-14). The sepak takraw ball is six bands on the six great circles that pass through the corners of an icosahedron.
- **Gear: our own drawings, none copied.** An earlier version used [Noto Emoji](https://github.com/googlefonts/noto-emoji), Pictogrammers MDI, three Wikimedia Commons files and one game-icons.net icon for eleven pieces, and sixteen Material Symbols icons for the awards, a cup and eleven sport pictograms. All of them have been redrawn as original drawings in this package's own style, and the third-party files, the scripts that fetched them and their licence texts were removed (see the CHANGELOG).
- **Gear drawn:** every piece of gear, made by eye from what the object is known to look like: they are not to scale (the true relative sizes are the balls', in `lineup`), and no rulebook's, catalogue's or icon set's drawing was copied. The Japanese items (the war fan, the sumo belt, the bamboo sword, the kendo helmet, the judo suit, the belt, the long bow, the arrow and the karate suit) are drawn the same way, and the words for them are those of the sports' governing bodies (see [japanese-sports.md](japanese-sports.md)).

## Judo and karate suits and belts

What the judogi, the karate-gi, the belts and the American football pictograms are drawn from. The drawings are our own (no photograph, brand mark or embroidery was copied); the rules and sources below fix the cut, the colours and the construction. Read 2026-10-14.

- **The judogi: IJF Sport and Organisation Rules, version 8 July 2020, section C1 "IJF Judogi Rules"** (the IJF's own text, [IJF_SOR.pdf as hosted by MIT](https://web.media.mit.edu/~reilly/IJF_SOR.pdf); the 2018 edition of the same section is [IJF-JUDOGI-RULES-2018](https://judovictoria.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/IJF-JUDOGI-RULES-2018-r.pdf)).
  - **C1.1:** the judogi is a jacket and trousers worn with a belt.
  - **C1.9 colour:** "The jacket and the trousers should be of a uniform colour": white ("snow white") or blue (Pantone 285M at most, 286M at least). `JUDOGI_WHITE` and `JUDOGI_BLUE` are our hexadecimal values between those references.
  - **C1.10 size, jacket:** the skirt covers the buttocks completely; the sleeves cover the whole arm including the wrist bone (the head of the ulna); the lapels cross at belt level at least 20 cm apart (the 2020 text; a later edition is reported to raise this to 25 cm, which a copy of the 2025 rules hosted by the Finnish federation could not be fetched to confirm); the lapel is 4 cm wide and at most 1 cm thick; the crossing point of the lapels is less than 10 cm below the top of the sternum. The lapels cross left over right, as in every gi.
  - **C1.10 size, trousers:** they end at most 5 cm from the ankle bone, and stand 10 to 15 cm clear of the leg at the knee.
  - **C1.10 belt:** 4 to 5 mm thick, with the tips 20 to 30 cm from the central knot, "not made of a stiff and/or slipping material", the knot "correctly and tightly made".
  - **C1.10 sleeves (the 2020 text, the 2018 text says the same in fewer words):** "The length of the sleeves must completely cover the wrist bone (head of the ulna)", and with the arms level the sokuteiki, the gauge the judges use, "must slip inside entirely and smoothly inside the sleeves", which is why the cuff is drawn loose. The owner's wording, "no more than 5 cm above the wrist with the arm held out forward", is not in either text read (5 cm is the trousers' figure there), so the drawing follows the text: the cuff covers the wrist. A judogi has no short or three-quarter sleeves; `sport:judo` and `gear:judogi` draw the sleeves hanging out and down to the hip, where the wrist is with the arms down, as long as the jacket (`test/judo.test.ts` measures it from the drawn paths).
  - **C1.8 backnumber:** sewn on the back, its top fixed horizontally and centrally 3 cm from the bottom of the collar, stitched round its edge with an X or six horizontal lines. The back view, `gear:judogi-back`, draws it: see the next section.
  - The quilted upper body, the stitched lapel rows, the side vents, the drawstring and the knee panels are not rules: they are what judogi makers' own catalogues show, drawn by eye.
- **The karate-gi: WKF Kumite Competition Rules and Kata Competition Rules, valid from 1.1.2023, article 2.2.1** ([Kumite](https://www.fpk.com.br/regulamentos/wkf-kumite.pdf), [Kata](https://www.fpk.com.br/regulamentos/wkf-kata.pdf)): a white karategi without stripes, piping or personal embroidery; (e) a red (AKA) or blue (AO) belt, no markings but the maker's label; (f) the belt about 5 cm wide with 15 cm free on each side of the knot, no longer than three-quarters of the thigh; (g) the jacket, tied with the belt, covers the hips and reaches at most three-quarters of the thigh; (i) jackets without ties cannot be used; (j) the sleeves reach at most the bend of the wrist and at least halfway down the forearm; (l) the trousers cover at least two thirds of the shin and stop above the ankle bone.
- **The belt's knot:** a flat square (reef) knot, drawn from the step-by-step tying diagrams the owner supplied for reference (wrapped twice, crossed at the back, both ends to the front, left over right then right over left); the IJF's wording is "correctly and tightly made" (C1.10). `gear:obi-step-1` to `gear:obi-step-6` ([obi-steps.md](obi-steps.md)) draw those steps in the order the owner listed them: centre the belt on the belly, wrap it twice round the waist, cross the ends in front, tuck one end up under both layers, tie the flat square knot (hon-musubi), finish with both ends hanging even. Which end goes over and which is tucked is not fixed by any rule read, so the drawings choose the wearer's left end over the other; the jacket they show is `gear:judogi`'s, left front over the right (migi-mae).
- **Grades and belt colours:**
  - **[IJF, "The belt" (judo culture)](https://www.ijf.org/history/judo-culture/2250):** the kyu run from the white belt (the highest number) to the brown belt (1st kyu); 1st to 5th dan wear black; 6th to 8th dan wear a red-and-white belt, "a belt with wide, alternating red and white bands" (introduced in 1931); 9th and 10th dan wear red (9th dan authorised in 1943). Two-tone intermediate belts (white-yellow, yellow-orange, orange-green, green-blue) "have been introduced in certain countries", in France in 1990. The page does not say how the two colours are laid out on the belt.
  - **[Rank in judo (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rank_in_judo):** the Kodokan's original kyu were light blue (6th), white (5th and 4th) and brown (3rd to 1st; purple for juniors), the other colours being added later by Koizumi (1926, England) and Kawaishi (1935, France); the red-and-white belt of the IJF has blocks of 20 cm (6th dan), 15 cm (7th) and 10 cm (8th), which the page says "doesn't seem to apply" in Japan. Our red-and-white belt has panels of roughly 21 cm, as makers sell it ("end panels measure 21 cm", from the owner's reference photograph of a shop listing, which also gives the belt as 42 mm wide); no federation text was found that fixes the width, and the IJF text above fixes only the thickness and the length of the tips.
  - **The order of the kyu colours** (white, yellow, orange, green, blue, brown) is the usual one of the countries that use them (British Judo's and France's schemes; the Kodokan's own kyu belts are white and brown only), so `OBI_RANKS` is a convention and not a Kodokan rule.
- **The women's black belt:** in Japan, women's black belts carry a white stripe along the middle of the belt: "women ... were compelled to sport white striped black belts that differentiated them from the male players ... the white line running through the center of the black belt" (Nihon Joshi Taiiku Daigaku Kiyo, "The Paradox of the White Striped Judo Black Belt", received 2020-12-10, [nichijo_kiyo51-03.pdf](https://jwcpe.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/1040/files/nichijo_kiyo51-03.pdf)). The paper says foreign women wear ordinary black belts; one secondary profile reports that the All Japan Judo Federation abolished the stripe in 2017, which could not be confirmed from a primary source. `women: true` is therefore a Japanese convention, not an IJF rule, and only the black belt takes it.
- **Two-colour belts:** which pairs, in which order, and since when: the IJF page above (white-yellow, yellow-orange, orange-green, green-blue; France 1990). British Judo's Mon grade scheme for ages 8 to 17 uses single colours with tags, and some clubs add stripes instead of two-tone belts; no source found describes the layout, so the belts are drawn as two lengthwise halves, which is how retailers show them. A blue-brown belt is drawn for completeness (the owner's list) though no source found names it.
- **American football:** the CFL ball has a 1-inch white stripe all the way round near each end (CFL Official Playing Rules, Rule 1, Section 2); the IFAF ball has "two 1-inch white stripes that are 3 to 3¼ inches from the end of the ball and located only on the two panels adjacent to the laces", with "one set of eight equally spaced lacings" (IFAF Football Rules and Interpretations, 2023 edition, Rule 1, Section 3, Article 1 e and c; [the 2023 text](https://amerikanskeidretter.no/wp-content/uploads/IFAF-Football-Rules-and-Interpretations-med-norske-tilpasninger-2023.pdf)); the NFL ball has no stripes (NFL Rulebook, Rule 2). The pictograms are drawn from our own `ball:football*` drawings; they show six rungs of laces and not eight, to read at 16 pixels.

## The IJF back number

What `gear:judogi-back` and `gear:judogi-blue-back` are drawn from; the page that describes them is [judogi-back.md](judogi-back.md). The drawing is our own: no IJF, partner or sponsor mark, and no photograph, was copied or traced. The patch's proportions were read off the two pictures below by eye. Read 2026-10-14.

- **The rule: IJF Sport and Organisation Rules, 8 July 2020, C1.8 "Backnumber"** ([IJF_SOR.pdf as hosted by MIT](https://web.media.mit.edu/~reilly/IJF_SOR.pdf), page 83 to 84; the same rule in the 2018 edition, C1.8, [IJF-JUDOGI-RULES-2018](https://judovictoria.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/IJF-JUDOGI-RULES-2018-r.pdf), page 75). What it fixes:
  - **What goes on it:** "their last (family) name and National Olympic Committee abbreviation" (the three letters, such as `CAN`, `JPN`, `USA`). The letters of the family name must be the ones registered in judobase; a mark on a letter is allowed (Müller for Muller), a different letter is not (Weiss, not Weiß). The name on the accreditation card must match the one on the backnumber (C1.1.4, C1.13).
  - **Colour:** blue for everyone; "Olympic champions are entitled to wear a gold backnumber and world champions a red one", only while the title is held, and back to blue within a month of losing it. This is the `band` option.
  - **Where:** "the top of the backnumber must be fixed horizontally and centrally three (3) cm from the bottom of the collar".
  - **Sewing:** "stitched around the entire perimeter and the lower and upper edges should be crossed to form an X or alternatively six (6) straight equidistant parallel horizontal lines"; for the Olympic Games the pattern is set by the IJF. The drawing stitches the perimeter only, as a dashed line.
  - **Publicity:** "During the competitions organised by IJF, it must include the publicity of the competition (which is different for white and blue judogi)". The drawing leaves that area blank.
  - **Where to buy:** "an official IJF backnumber partner", www.mybacknumber.com and www.officialbacknumber.com (2018: ijfbacknumber.com).
- **The size and the layout: the IJF partner's own manual**, "Official Backnumber and Sponsorlabel Manual", page 3, "International Judo Federation backnumber rules" (mybacknumber.com, [English PDF](https://mybacknumber.com/files/news/3f725766ca75d0c8f2cf337f133ed441.pdf), [German PDF](https://mybacknumber.com/files/news/2c501a3c883f5f208c030b343f491b83.pdf), linked from [mybacknumber.com/manual](https://mybacknumber.com/manual)):
  - **Large size, 38 cm wide by 39 cm high:** women -63, -70, -78 and +78 kg; men -66, -73, -81, -90, -100 and +100 kg. **Small size, 34 by 35 cm:** women -40, -44, -48, -52 and -57 kg; men -50, -55 and -60 kg. The drawing is the large one (the two have the same proportions within 1%).
  - **The layout it draws, top to bottom:** a white patch; a coloured band across the top with the family name in white capitals (the band's edges are drawn arched); the three-letter code under it in bold coloured capitals; the rest of the patch is "SPONSOR", blank space; a very small square label (the authenticity code) at the left edge level with the code, and a small "(c)" notice on the right. The drawing keeps the square and leaves out the notice, which is the IJF's mark.
  - **The reserve judogi's** back number has "WORLD JUDO TOUR" in place of the name and code and "a free space of 15 x 32 cm for affixing the sponsor's label". Not drawn.
  - **Lettering:** bold sans-serif capitals, the name condensed; no typeface is named. The drawing uses the package's own stroke letters (`ball.letters.ts`), set condensed for the name.
  - **No flag:** neither the rule nor the manual shows a national flag on the patch (the country is the three letters), so none is drawn, and the `flags` option, which the printed pucks take, is not used here.
- **Not confirmed, and so not claimed:** the band's exact blue (the manual's picture shows a dark royal blue; `#26318f` is our own choice), the band's and the letters' heights and margins (read off the pictures; the sources give only the outer size), the size of the sponsor area (the manual draws it as the lower part of the patch), the typeface, whether the blue judogi's patch differs from the white judogi's other than in the publicity (the rule says the publicity differs; the manual's picture shows one patch), the gold and red bands' exact colours (named, not shown), and the 2025 edition of C1.8 (the 2018 and 2020 editions were read; a later one could not be fetched). The two pictures in the rules show a real athlete's name and a real sponsor on a patch; neither is reproduced.
- **Licence:** the rules and the manual are documents of the IJF and of mybacknumber.com. Only facts (sizes, positions, colours named, the wording of a rule in a short quotation) were taken; nothing was copied.

## Soccer balls

What the soccer balls with other panel counts, and the indoor ones, are made from; the page that describes them is [soccer-balls.md](soccer-balls.md). Every construction is a geometric derivation, written for this package and checked by `test/ball.test.ts` (vertices, edges and faces counted, Euler's formula); no brand's or tournament's ball, pattern or graphics was looked at or traced, and none is used.

- **32 panels:** the truncated icosahedron, 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons ([Truncated icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_icosahedron)).
- **26 panels:** the rhombicuboctahedron, an Archimedean solid with 8 triangles, 18 squares, 48 edges and 24 vertices, the permutations of (±1, ±1, ±(1 + √2)) ([Rhombicuboctahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombicuboctahedron)). Read 2026-10-14.
- **12 panels:** the regular dodecahedron, vertices (±1, ±1, ±1) and the cyclic permutations of (0, ±1/φ, ±φ) ([Regular dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_dodecahedron)); its pentagons are about the 12 vertex directions of the icosahedron, (±1, 0, ±φ) and cyclic permutations. The four-colouring of those 12 directions is found by search.
- **18 panels:** no polyhedron to cite. It is derived: the six faces of a cube, each cut into three strips at ±1/3 and projected onto the sphere from the centre, so that every seam is an arc of a great circle; the layout is the one `ball:volleyball` uses. No source for a historical 18-panel ball's geometry was found, so none is claimed.
- **14 panels:** derived from the truncated cube, whose vertices are the permutations of (±ξ, ±1, ±1) (regular for ξ = √2 − 1; [Truncated cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_cube)) and which has 8 triangles and 6 octagons. The 12 edges between octagons are replaced by S-curves, offset from the great-circle arc by 0.2 × chord × sin(2πt) × (0.5 + 0.5 sin(πt)), a curve that is its own image under a half turn about the edge's middle, so the cube's 24 rotations are kept: 6 propellers (the octagons) and 8 triangles. ξ = 0.42, nearly the regular one, chosen by looking at the contact sheets. The S-curve is this package's own.
- **6 panels, thermobonded:** this package's own design: six gores between seams that run from pole to pole, longitude = 360° × i / 6 + 90° × latitude / 90° (a spherical lune twisted through 180° of longitude), the poles tilted 28° from the spin axis. Thermobonded means glued, not stitched, so the seams are drawn as shallow grooves.

### The indoor balls' rules

Read 2026-10-14, in the text of the rule itself.

## Vintage, era-style and patterned balls

What the fifty balls of [vintage-balls.md](vintage-balls.md) are made from. The constructions are facts or our own geometry; the graphics on them are drawn here and are not any real ball's. Read 2026-10-14.

### Vintage soccer balls

- **Leather over a bladder, laced:** [Wikipedia, "Ball (association football)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_(association_football)): earlier balls were an inflated animal bladder, later covered in leather; vulcanised rubber made inflatable bladders; the leather casing was stitched with "a lace of the same material (known as *tiento* in Spanish)"; the 1930 final used a 12-panel ball and the 1950 ball is captioned as a 12-panel one; and the modern 32-panel design "was developed in 1962 by Eigil Nielsen" (also [The Truncated Icosahedron as an Inflatable Ball](https://pp.bme.hu/ar/article/download/12375/8158), which says it was introduced by his firm Select in 1962 and never patented; and [Wikipedia, "Football (ball)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_(ball)): the first 32-panel ball was sold by Select in Denmark and became common in continental Europe in the 1960s).
- **18 strips (`ball:soccer-1930-18-panel`):** the same article says most balls of the rubber-bladder era "had tanned leather with eighteen sections stitched together", "arranged in six panels of three strips each". The article does not date the 18-strip ball to the 1930s; the date is the request's. The construction is `strips18` of `ball.family.panelled.ts` (the volleyball's layout).
- **8 long panels with a button or cap at each end (`ball:soccer-1870-hand-stitched`, `ball:soccer-1900-laced`):** the National Football Museum's catalogue lists late-nineteenth-century and circa-1912 balls of eight leather panels, one with round cap ends and laces ([Children's football, late 19th century](https://www.nationalfootballmuseum.com/collections_detail/childrens-football-late-19th-century/) and [Football, circa 1912](https://www.nationalfootballmuseum.com/collections_detail/football-circa-1912/)). Those pages answered 403 to our fetch, so they were read as search-result excerpts and not in full. The eight gores are `gores(8, 0)` (meridians from pole to pole); the button and the cap, and the lace slit with its eyelets and criss-crossed lace, are our own. A history of rugby balls gives hand-stitched four-panel casings from 1870 ([Wikipedia, "Football (ball)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_(ball)), Rugby football); the sources on panel counts for early association balls disagree, so no count is claimed beyond the museum's.
- **12 panels (`ball:soccer-1950-12-panel`):** the dodecahedron of `ball.tilings.ts`, as for `ball:soccer-12` (the 1950 ball is captioned as 12-panel in the article above).
- **Size:** the Football Association's 1872 revision of the Laws gave a circumference of 27 to 28 inches (68.6 to 71.1 cm), the figure used for the four older balls ([Wikipedia, "Ball (association football)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_(association_football)) and a timeline that agrees; secondary sources, the Laws' own 1872 text was not read). The mass the Laws gave in the early years is not drawn into `size`, because the secondary sources read disagree on the figure and the year.
- **Leather and thread:** tan `#b98a55`, brown `#8a5a30`, chestnut `#9c4f26` and `#7a3a1c` and the chestnut and tan of the 1960s ball are colours of our own choosing; the scuffs are patches laid by a seeded function (`scuffs` in `ball.decal.ts`), not a photograph.

### Soccer balls in the style of an era

- **Panel counts:** the article on the match balls of the men's global tournament (Wikipedia, read 2026-10-14) gives 32 panels for the 1970 ball (a black and white one, 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons), 14 for 2006, 8 for 2010, six thermally bonded polyurethane panels for 2014 and 20 for 2022; it states no count for the others. Our 1978, 1986, 1998 and 2002 balls are on the 32 panels, because those balls' designs sat on the classic panelling; our 2018 ball is on six panels, the same thermobonded layout as 2014, which is our choice (the article states no count for 2018).
- **The look:** each graphic is drawn here to evoke a period and to differ from it. Curved triangles in threes (the late-1970s, 1980s and 1990s balls) are three leaves of a spiral on each hexagon (`tongue` and `spiral` in `ball.decal.ts`), vary in weight, colour and ornament, and are our shapes; the triangular sweep, the contour lines of the 8-panel ball, the ribbons, the pixel blocks and the nested outlines of the 20-panel ball are our own. No real design, mark or name is used or reproduced, and the photographs the package's author supplied were looked at for the general look of each era and not traced.
- **8 panels:** an octahedron whose twelve edges are bent into S curves with the curve of the 14-panel layout (`octahedron8`); **20 panels:** the icosahedron, with its 12 vertices the cyclic permutations of (0, ±1, ±φ) ([Regular icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_icosahedron)); the triangles are four-coloured by search like the 12-panel ball's pentagons.
- **Size:** IFAB Laws of the Game, Law 2, as for every 11-a-side ball here.

### Patterns of a shop wall

Generic and our own: a five-pointed star, nested hexagon outlines, flame tongues (a spine that spirals, a width that grows and then falls to a point), chevrons (a band whose centre is lower than its ends, repeated on each of eight gores), a two-tone swirl (four gores twisted through 330 degrees) and a metallic finish (the metallic material of the engine: sky above, dark horizon, bounce below). The colourways are our own colours. Suggested by a photograph of a shop wall supplied by the package's author; nothing copied.

### Baseballs

- **Lemon-peel and figure-8:** [Wikipedia, "Baseball (ball)"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_(ball)): the mid-nineteenth-century "lemon peel ball" named for "its distinct four lines of stitching design"; the figure-8 cover credited by some to Ellis Drake and by others to William A. Cutler (who sold the design to William Harwood in 1858); 108 double stitches on a regulation ball. The lemon-peel ball is `gores(4, 0)`, four meridian seams meeting at two points, with a cross stitch.
- **Red and blue; black and red:** the same article quotes Bob Feller, a 1930s rookie: "National League baseball laces were black, intertwined with red; American League baseball laces were blue and red". We draw alternate stitches of the two colours; that is an interpretation of "intertwined", not a record of the exact thread.
- **Aged leather:** a tea-brown tone (`#d8c08e`), scuffs by `scuffs` and a faded red thread: our own colours. The same article notes that a ball discolours with dirt and use.
- **11 in optic softball:** WBSC Official Rules of Softball Slow Pitch 2026-2029, Appendix 3, read in full: B.2 "The completed 27.9 cm. (11 in.) ball shall be between 27.6 cm. (10 7/8 in.) and 28.3 cm. (11 1/8 in.) in circumference and shall weigh between 166.5 g. (5 7/8 ounces) and 173.6 g. (6 1/8 ounces). The flat seam style shall have not less than 80 stitches in each cover", and the standards table (colour "White or Yellow Optic", thread "Red stitch").

### Volleyballs in national colours

The 18-panel layout of `ball:volleyball` with three colours taken from a country's flag. The colours are ours, chosen to read as the flag's (Brazil's green `#009c3b`, yellow `#ffdf00` and blue `#002776` are the ones its flag is usually given in; the others are close approximations, not read from a national text). A flag of two colours (Japan, Canada, Poland) takes a darker shade of one as its third. The size is `ball:volleyball`'s, from the FIVB rule. How the colours sit on the ball is ours too: each country has its own layout of the 18 panels (a band, a split, a field, stripes), so that balls with alike colours still look different. Real national-team match balls were looked for in public product photographs and none could be confirmed, so only the idea of the flag's own structure is borrowed (Poland's white above red, Serbia's bands from the top down, Italy's green, white and red in turn), and no maker's mark or name.
