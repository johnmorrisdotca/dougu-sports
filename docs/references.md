# References

What the emblems are drawn from, and where the figures were read. The notice is in [NOTICE.md](../NOTICE.md); nothing here is copied, so no third-party licence is kept in [LICENSES/](../LICENSES/).

## Trophies, medals and crests

The trophies are drawn in SVG for this package, in a simplified and generic form, from what the real objects are known to look like: their proportions and their parts, looked at in photographs that were never traced or copied (the contact sheets were compared with them by eye). Each famous trophy is described, not named: `trophy:hockey-cup` is a tall silver cup with a small flared bowl, a collar, and a barrel of five bands that each widen a little; `trophy:baseball` a ring of thirty gold pennants on poles, their tops stepping up once round, standing round a baseball on a round base; `trophy:football` a silver football leaning on one end on a tapered three-sided stand; `trophy:basketball` a gold ball over a rim and a net, on a tapered column and a stepped base; `trophy:canadian-bowl` a wide silver bowl with two handles on a stem over a tall conical base of panels; `trophy:soccer-globe` a gold globe held up by figures that spiral round a column that flares from a slim waist, over two green bands; `trophy:tennis-plate` a silver salver with rings of gilt and rounded panels; `trophy:tennis-cup` a gilded two-handled cup with a domed lid and a small pineapple; `trophy:cricket-urn` a small terracotta urn with square handles on a wooden base; `trophy:rugby-cup` a gilded two-handled cup with a lid. No ids, titles, docs or drawings carry a real trophy's name, logo, wordmark or engraving.

The medals' ribbon has five stripes in the colours of a games' five rings (blue, yellow, black, green, red), and no rings are drawn.

## Balls and gear

What the balls and implements are drawn from, and how the gear is drawn.

- **Sizes: the rulebooks.** For each ball the federation's own rulebook (not Wikipedia, not a shop) was read for the diameter or length, the circumference, the mass and what the rule fixes about the look, and the rule is cited in the ball's `size.rule`. The table is in NOTICE.md. Read 2026-10-14. Figures that are not in the rule are marked: the basketball 3x3's (the FIBA 3x3 appendix refused the request; the figures are a retailer's reproduction of its Table 1), the football of the IFAF (in a diagram, read from the rendered page), the Australian football's length (the Laws give two girths; 280 mm is the length of an ellipse of those), the hammer's wire (the table gives only a maximum overall length), the sepak takraw ball (the 2013 ISTAF law, the newest found), the padel ball (FIP's certification text that quotes the Rules of Padel), the softball (the WBSC Slow Pitch appendix read in English, the Fast Pitch one in the FFBS's French translation) and the water polo ball (World Aquatics' regulations, hosted by a national federation).
- **Where the rule says less than the brief did:** the pickleball rulebook does not tell indoor and outdoor balls apart (both are 26 to 40 holes; 26 large holes and 40 small ones is how they are sold); the cricket Laws set no colour (the ICC one-day conditions say white; no ICC text read says pink); the NFL ball is "natural tan", with laces and no stripes in the text; the CFL ball has 1-inch white stripes round the short axis, three inches from the widest point; the IFAF ball has two such stripes on the two panels beside the laces, eight laces and four panels; the padel ball may be white, yellow or any contrasting colour; the water polo ball's colour is not regulated; the squash specification names double yellow, yellow, green, red and blue dots and recommends them; the handball Ball Regulations ask for at least 32 panels on a leather ball; table-tennis balls are white or orange and matt; the discus has no indentations; a golf ball has no minimum mass and no maximum size.
- **The look: our own.** The soccer ball is the truncated icosahedron (12 pentagons, 20 hexagons) with the edges followed along the sphere; its coloured twin and the teqball ball have a motif and colours of our own, and no World Cup ball's pattern is used. The baseball, softball, tennis, padel and sliotar seam is the parametric curve x=(1-b)cos t + b cos 3t, y=(1-b)sin t - b sin 3t, z=2sqrt(b(1-b)) sin 2t (it lies on the unit sphere for every b). The volleyball's panels are the six faces of a cube, three strips each, mapped onto the sphere so that every seam is an arc of a great circle and the strips of neighbouring faces run square to each other; the 8-panel ball is the octants. The basketball's two curved channels, latitude = +-(0.304 + 0.585 sin^2 longitude), were fitted by least squares to the 1,766 seam pixels of a drawing and a photograph of a generic ball, which were looked at and not copied (a Wikimedia Commons clip art and a photograph, viewed on 2026-10-14). The sepak takraw ball is six bands on the six great circles that pass through the corners of an icosahedron.
- **Gear: our own drawings, none copied.** An earlier version used [Noto Emoji](https://github.com/googlefonts/noto-emoji), Pictogrammers MDI, three Wikimedia Commons files and one game-icons.net icon for eleven pieces, and sixteen Material Symbols icons for the awards, a cup and eleven sport pictograms. All of them have been redrawn as original drawings in this package's own style, and the third-party files, the scripts that fetched them and their licence texts were removed (see the CHANGELOG).
- **Gear drawn:** every piece of gear, made by eye from what the object is known to look like: they are not to scale (the true relative sizes are the balls', in `lineup`), and no rulebook's, catalogue's or icon set's drawing was copied. The Japanese items (the war fan, the sumo belt, the bamboo sword, the kendo helmet, the judo suit, the belt, the long bow, the arrow and the karate suit) are drawn the same way, and the words for them are those of the sports' governing bodies (see [japanese-sports.md](japanese-sports.md)).

## Soccer balls

What the soccer balls with other panel counts, and the indoor ones, are made from; the page that describes them is [soccer-balls.md](soccer-balls.md). Every construction is a geometric derivation, written for this package and checked by `test/ball.test.ts` (vertices, edges and faces counted, Euler's formula); no brand's or tournament's ball, pattern or graphics was looked at or traced, and none is used.

- **32 panels:** the truncated icosahedron, 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons ([Truncated icosahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_icosahedron)).
- **26 panels:** the rhombicuboctahedron, an Archimedean solid with 8 triangles, 18 squares, 48 edges and 24 vertices, the permutations of (±1, ±1, ±(1 + √2)) ([Rhombicuboctahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhombicuboctahedron)). Read 2026-10-14.
- **12 panels:** the regular dodecahedron, vertices (±1, ±1, ±1) and the cyclic permutations of (0, ±1/φ, ±φ) ([Regular dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_dodecahedron)); its pentagons are about the 12 vertex directions of the icosahedron, (±1, 0, ±φ) and cyclic permutations. The four-colouring of those 12 directions is found by search.
- **18 panels:** no polyhedron to cite. It is derived: the six faces of a cube, each cut into three strips at ±1/3 and projected onto the sphere from the centre, so that every seam is an arc of a great circle; the layout is the one `ball:volleyball` uses. No source for a historical 18-panel ball's geometry was found, so none is claimed.
- **14 panels:** derived from the truncated cube, whose vertices are the permutations of (±ξ, ±1, ±1) (regular for ξ = √2 − 1; [Truncated cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_cube)) and which has 8 triangles and 6 octagons. The 12 edges between octagons are replaced by S-curves, offset from the great-circle arc by 0.2 × chord × sin(2πt) × (0.5 + 0.5 sin(πt)), a curve that is its own image under a half turn about the edge's middle, so the cube's 24 rotations are kept: 6 propellers (the octagons) and 8 triangles. ξ = 0.42, nearly the regular one, chosen by looking at the contact sheets. The S-curve is this package's own.
- **6 panels, thermobonded:** this package's own design: six gores between seams that run from pole to pole, longitude = 360° × i / 6 + 90° × latitude / 90° (a spherical lune twisted through 180° of longitude), the poles tilted 28° from the spin axis. Thermobonded means glued, not stitched, so the seams are drawn as shallow grooves.

### The indoor balls' rules

Read 2026-10-14, in the text of the rule itself.

- **Futsal:** Futsal Laws of the Game 2025/26 (FIFA), Law 2 The Ball, section 1 Qualities and measurements: spherical, made of suitable material, circumference 62 to 64 cm, 400 to 440 g at the start of the match, pressure 0.6 to 0.9 atm (600 to 900 g/cm²) at sea level, and the ball must not bounce lower than 50 cm or higher than 65 cm on the first rebound when dropped from 2 m (<https://digitalhub.fifa.com/m/20d52e6779b53321/original/FUTSAL-Laws-of-the-Game-2025-2026.pdf>). Size 4 is the name the trade gives that circumference; the Laws give the circumference.
- **Beach soccer:** Beach Soccer Laws of the Game (FIFA) 2024-25, Law 2 The Ball, section 1: circumference 68 to 70 cm, 400 to 440 g at the start of the match, pressure 0.4 to 0.6 atm (400 to 600 g/cm²) at sea level; the same figures are in the 2021/22 edition (<https://www.the-aiff.com/media/uploads/2024/11/Beach-Soccer-Laws-of-the-Game-2024-25.pdf>, <https://digitalhub.fifa.com/m/4ff207e04394d6f9/original/-Beach-Soccer-Laws-of-the-game-2021-2022.pdf>). The Law sets no bounce. The AIFF-hosted copy is the 2024-25 text.
- **The 11-a-side ball** that the constructions share is IFAB Laws of the Game, Law 2 (68 to 70 cm, 410 to 450 g), as for `ball:soccer`.
- **Colourways:** winter orange (`#ff6a13`) and high-visibility yellow (`#e8f21c`) are colours of this package's choosing, for balls that show against snow and in low light; the Laws do not fix a colour.
